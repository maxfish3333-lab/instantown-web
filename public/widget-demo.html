10 hidden gems in Trastevere you can't miss

By Elena Rossi  ·  8 min read
[ photo: Trastevere at sunset ]

Tucked between the Tiber and the Janiculum Hill, Trastevere is one of Rome's most atmospheric neighbourhoods. Cobbled alleys, ivy-clad façades, trattorias spilling onto the street — this is the Rome that visitors come looking for and locals still call home.

Whether you arrive in the golden hour or stumble in after midnight, Trastevere rewards the curious. Here are ten spots our editors visit every time they're in the city.

"Trastevere is the kind of place where you get lost on purpose." — anonymous traveller, 2024

Start your morning at the Basilica di Santa Maria in Trastevere before the crowds arrive. The 12th-century mosaics catch the early light in a way that photographs can never fully capture. From there, weave south toward Piazza Trilussa and grab a coffee at any of the small bars along the way.

For lunch, skip the tourist menus and look for handwritten signs in Italian — a reliable indicator that the kitchen hasn't pivoted to English-language expectations. Cacio e pepe, carbonara, supplì: the classics hold up for a reason.

In the afternoon, climb to the Passeggiata del Gianicolo. The panorama takes in the entire city from the Colosseum to the dome of Saint Peter's. Cannon fires at noon — an old tradition still kept alive.

As evening falls, Trastevere transforms. The narrow streets fill with diners, street musicians and slow-walking couples. Book nothing; wander everything.
